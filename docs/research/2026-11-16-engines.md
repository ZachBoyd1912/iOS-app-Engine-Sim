### 2. Real-world ranges

**Volumetric efficiency (VE) vs rpm**
- Wikipedia gives no VE equation and no curve shape. It says the limit for naturally aspirated engines is "about 130%" (experimental), and forced induction can exceed 100% ([Volumetric efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volumetric_efficiency)). That figure is an outlier, not a typical value.
- A WOT dyno example on a 2.0 L NA Ford reads 86% VE; a 1.8 L example suggests about 86–88% real-world at peak torque ([EPI](https://epi-eng.com/piston_engine_technology/volumetric_efficiency.htm), search snippet, weak source).
- Typical modern NA: roughly 85–95% peak at torque rpm, falling off at both ends. Peak VE moves toward higher rpm with variable valve timing ([Volumetric efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volumetric_efficiency)). Curve coefficients: **UNVERIFIED**.
- Suggested model (derived): VE(rpm) = VE_peak × exp(−((rpm − rpm_peak)/σ)²), with rpm_peak near torque peak and σ roughly 0.3–0.5 × rpm_peak. Parameters need calibration.

**Mass airflow (MAF) sensor range**
- Bosch HFM 5 catalogue variants, in kg/h: 8–370, 10–480, 12–640, 12–850, and 15–1000, accuracy ≤3% ([Bosch HFM5 sheet, rennlist-hosted](https://rennlist.com/forums/attachments/944-turbo-and-turbo-s-forum/264972d1206039416-home-made-maf-bosch_hfm5_sensors.pdf)).
- Later HFM 5 part 1928300599: −50 to 1200 kg/h, 3%, pressure drop under 15 hPa ([Xonlink-hosted Bosch PDF](https://xonstorage.blob.core.windows.net/pdf/bosch_1928300599_apr22_xonlink.pdf)).
- Convert to g/s by dividing kg/h by 3.6. For example, 370 kg/h ≈ 103 g/s and 1200 kg/h ≈ 333 g/s.
- Enthusiast figures: stock 1G DSM sensor about 210 g/s; GM about 500 g/s; HP Tuners scaling 0–512 g/s ([DSM forum mirror](https://www.ecmtuning.com/wiki/maffrequencylimits?do=edit), weak source).
- Airflow formula (derived, checked against the 2.0 L example): ṁ [kg/s] = VE × ρ × V_d × (rpm/120), with V_d in m³ and ρ ≈ 1.2 kg/m³. Example: 2.0 L, 6000 rpm, VE 1.0 gives 120 g/s.

**Injector sizing**
- Lb/hr per injector = HP × BSFC / (N_inj × DC). Cc/min = lb/hr × 10.5. Duty cycle target 80%, with some sources saying 80–90% and Holley recommending a 70% maximum for a mild NA street engine ([Injector sizing sources](https://diyautotune.com/blogs/tech-corner/fuel-injector-selection), [search snippet](https://www.thetuningschool.com/blogs/news/calculating-injector-size), weak sources).
- Check of the 10.5 factor (derived): 1 lb/hr at gasoline density 0.74 kg/L ≈ 10.2 cc/min. The 10.5 figure is a rounded rule of thumb.
- Worked examples (mine): 500 HP, BSFC 0.60, 8 injectors, 80% gives 46.9 lb/hr ≈ 492 cc/min. 500 HP, BSFC 0.50, 8 injectors gives ≈ 410 cc/min.
- One source's 4-cylinder figure (750 cc/min at 500 HP) is wrong; the formula gives ≈ 984 cc/min.

**BSFC**
- Wikipedia: gasoline cycle average 322 g/(kW·h); best production gasoline about 225 g/(kW·h) (0.370 lb/hp·h); diesel down to about 198 g/(kW·h). Conversion: lb/(hp·h) = g/(kW·h) × 0.001644 ([BSFC, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brake-specific_fuel_consumption)).
- 322 g/(kW·h) ≈ 0.53 lb/(hp·h) (derived).
- Enthusiast tables: NA gasoline about 0.5; boosted gasoline or E85 about 0.6–0.7 ([calculator mirror](https://www.powernationtv.com/calculators/fuel-injector-size-calculator), weak source).

**Nitrous oxide (N₂O)**
- Chemistry: N₂O → N₂ + ½O₂, exothermic. Oxygen is 36.36% by mass (16/44), vs 21% by volume in air ([Nitrous oxide engine, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrous_oxide_engine)). Wikipedia gives no power, AFR, or timing numbers.
- Forum guidance, anecdotal: dry kits suit about 50–125 HP; a dry shot above 100 HP needs injector and fuel-pump upgrades; above 125 HP needs stronger internals ([Mustang Forums and DSM forum threads](https://www.dsmtuners.com/threads/the-nitrous-faq.37223/post-322334)). Timing rule of thumb: about 2° retard per 50 HP of shot, often treated as a starting point only.
- AFR: one tuner suggests about 12.0 on the shot and tuning on a dyno. Treat nitrous as a boost source and run richer than the stock map ([HPAcademy forum](https://www.hpacademy.com/forum/general-tuning-discussion/show/tuning-nitrous-oxide-afrs/), weak).
- Holley's shot-size page returned HTTP 403, so its numbers are **UNVERIFIED**.
- Physics model (derived, not sourced): O₂ mass added per kg N₂O = 0.364. Fuel that stoichiometrically burns it = 0.364 / (0.232 × AFR_stoich) kg per kg N₂O (≈ 0.107 kg fuel per kg N₂O at AFR 14.7). Mass flow through the jet is choked-flow limited, ṁ ≈ Cd·A·√(2ρΔP). Calibrate Cd.

**Turbo vs supercharger boost vs rpm**
- Positive displacement (Roots, twin-screw): "almost constant level of boost pressure increase at all engine speeds" ([Supercharger, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercharger)). Roots efficiency 40–50% at high boost, marked [citation needed] ([Supercharger, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercharger)).
- Eaton Roots boost is bell-shaped in one enthusiast test, with lower boost at both ends ([HPAcademy/Holdener mirror](https://hpacademy.com/technical-articles/explained-selecting-a-supercharger), weak source). Twin-screw reached peak boost at about 6500 rpm in the same test ([Kenne Bell/Holdener](https://kennebell.net/boost-bash-part-2-richard-holdener-2), search snippet).
- Centrifugal: boost rises "exponentially with engine speed" above a threshold; efficiency 70–85%, marked [citation needed] ([Supercharger, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercharger)). The Vortech test figures were 1.7 psi at 2500 rpm and 11.3 psi at 6500 rpm ([Holdener mirror](https://kennebell.net/boost-bash-part-1-richard-holdener/)). A quadratic fit gives 1.7 × (6500/2500)² ≈ 11.5 psi, matching well. Suggested model (derived): ΔP ∝ (DR × N)².
- Turbo: boost onset depends on exhaust flow; "at low rpms, the exhaust gas flow rate is unable to spin the turbine sufficiently." Lag is the delay before boost ([Turbocharger, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbocharger)). No numeric boost-onset figures are given. Suggested model (derived): a turbo-shaft ODE, J·dω/dt = τ_turbine(exhaust flow) − τ_compressor(boost). Calibrate on real data.

### 3. Recommended approach for the sim

- Store firing data as data tables: crank-pin angles, firing order, and per-cylinder ignition angle θ_c = (position in firing order − 1) × 720 / N.
- Crank degrees to time: dθ/dt = 6 × rpm deg/s. Engine balance is a separate force model; do not derive vibration from firing data alone.
- Keep VE, MAF, injector, and nitrous models as calibratable parameters. Use the formulas in section 2 and expect to tune constants.
- Only use the W16 firing order after confirming it against Bugatti documentation, a teardown, or a peer-reviewed analysis.

### 4. Risks and gaps

- Unverified: W16 firing order and balance/sound; V10 90° intervals; flat-plane crank pin angles; Holley shot tables (403); the 2°/50 HP timing rule and AFR figures (forum only); VE curve shape (no dyno-backed source found).
- Wikipedia citation-needed tags: the 130% NA VE limit, Roots efficiency, centrifugal efficiency, and the W16 "two VR8" claim.
- Mirror and forum sources (Holdener, DSM, EngineLabs, WhichCar, CarsCounsel) are lower quality. Treat them as calibration hints, not specs.
- The 10.5 cc/min per lb/hr factor and the 80% duty cycle are rules of thumb. Real injector sizing needs manufacturer flow data and fuel-pressure correction.
